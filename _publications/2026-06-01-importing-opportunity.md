<p>Import competition is known to cause lasting income losses for manufacturing workers, but its effects on their children have been largely overlooked. One might expect these losses to make family background matter more for children’s income, since poorer families have little to draw on while richer ones can insulate theirs. Using matched employer-employee data that link children to their parents, I find the opposite: import competition from China increases income mobility among Canadian manufacturing families. In heavily exposed families, where a child ends up depends about 20% less on where their parents stood. Children from poorer families climb the income ladder; they escape by moving out of their parents’ industries into ones that offer similar pay on average but where family background matters less. Children from richer families fall; they stood to follow their parents into high-paying industries, and the shock takes those jobs away.</p>
